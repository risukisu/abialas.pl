title: "How I run marketing"
status: published
order: 1
featured: true
client: "Appsilon"
summary: "Three pillars you can measure — so marketing compounds instead of scrambles."
problem: >-
  When I took over, marketing mostly did things because we'd always done them.
  Plenty of activity, talented people — but ask "why are we doing this?" and the
  honest answer was "because we always have," or a shrug. There were no
  campaigns, no shared model for why any of it mattered, and it all leaned on
  organic inbound with no backup when that dipped.
approach: >-
  I gave the work a why. I brought in real campaigns — and the systems to
  deliver them — and organised the whole function around three pillars you can
  measure on their own terms: Brand (the demand-influence layer), Market Sensing
  (the strategic-intelligence layer), and Lead Gen (the growth layer). They
  behave like connected vessels — a stronger brand and a sharper read on the
  market make lead gen easier, and lead gen turns all of it into qualified
  pipeline. On top of that: result-based areas of responsibility with health
  KPIs, a content pipeline, SCRUM for cadence, and a written manual so the
  system outlives any one person. The habit underneath all of it — measure what
  we do, ask why, and put more weight on the parts that perform.
result: >-
  It added up to the best pipeline year in the company's history. In 2025 the
  wider business delivered roughly 150% of its new-business revenue target —
  marketing's job there was to build the brand and the pipeline that made it
  possible; sales and delivery carried it home. And it held on a deliberately
  strict bar: what we count as an MQL is closer to what most teams call an SQL —
  a real fit-and-intent buyer, not a form fill. I inherited a team and have
  spent the time since rebuilding and reorganising the function around it.
